# **Meeting Context**

* Informal discovery conversation with a physician (Clara) about clinical workflows  
* Focus: understanding where AI/tooling could have the most impact in a hospital setting

# **Pre-Charting: The Highest-Impact Problem**

* Pre-charting identified as the biggest pain point and best opportunity for tooling  
* Process involves reviewing 20-30 different resources per patient  
  * Takes 5-10 minutes per patient just to locate records  
  * Records are 5-10 pages, poorly formatted, with lots of irrelevant content  
  * Must skim everything to avoid missing critical details, then consolidate manually  
* Goal: extract high-yield information as fast as possible before seeing the patient  
* Standard checklist on admit:  
  * Look up medical record number in the EHR  
  * Check chief complaint (e.g., shortness of breath, chest pain, fall)  
  * Review vitals and acuity indicators  
  * Verify current medications against pharmacy fill history  
  * Get patient history directly once stable enough to speak

# **New vs. Returning Patients**

* Returning patients (same health system): much easier, can reference prior notes  
* New patients or out-of-state transfers: start from scratch, longer appointment slot  
  * Intake forms filled beforehand (where available) help streamline the visit  
* Inter-hospital records still largely transferred by fax (e.g., Cornell to Mount Sinai)  
  * Critical to avoid repeating workups already done elsewhere  
  * Initial vitals and labs before any intervention are especially important

# **Diagnosis and Clinical Decision Support**

* Most diagnoses are straightforward once initial labs and imaging are reviewed  
* Tools used for complex or obscure cases:  
  * Doximity: preferred, especially for medication regimens and differential diagnosis  
  * Open Evidence: improving but sometimes overstretches claims from research papers  
* Subspecialty consults (cardiology, endocrinology, vascular surgery) used when stuck  
  * Workflow: page the specialist, they call back

# **Medication Management and Comorbidities**

* Medication verification is tedious but critical  
  * Cross-reference EHR prescriptions with pharmacy fill recency  
  * Patients often unsure of what they actually take  
* Medications can be the cause of admission or create contraindications  
  * Example: stop blood thinners for a bleeding patient; hold antihypertensives for low BP  
* Comorbidities (e.g., diabetes \+ hypertension \+ kidney disease) significantly increase complexity  
  * Some medications address multiple conditions simultaneously  
  * Drug selection must account for interaction risks across all active conditions  
* Complex/comorbid cases are more prevalent in underserved communities (e.g., East Harlem)

# **Charting, Billing, and Tools**

* Live patient notes: brief (1-2 lines), mostly physician interpretation, not verbatim patient wording  
  * Some use AI transcription tools (e.g., MMEI) but editing can take longer than typing  
  * 15-30 minute appointments leave little time for live documentation  
* Billing handled separately; physician only marks visit level, consult involvement, and codes like CPAP  
* Epic is the primary EHR system  
  * Data-sharing between health systems is the real barrier, not Epic’s capabilities  
  * Hospitals are reluctant to share records across systems
